# X Hook Drafts

Every protein in your body — every enzyme, hormone, antibody — was built by a machine called the ribosome.

Here's what's wild: the ribosome doesn't use protein to build protein.

Its catalytic core is pure RNA. The ribosomal proteins? Structural scaffolding. They hold the shape. The actual chemistry — the bond that links amino acids together — is done by RNA.

This was confirmed by X-ray crystallography. The active site is protein-free. The nearest amino acid is more than 18 angstroms away. RNA does all the work.

What this means: life figured out how to make proteins before it had proteins to work with. The ribosome is a molecular fossil — a machine that hasn't needed to upgrade its engine in 4 billion years.

And every antibiotic that targets ribosomes (erythromycin, chloramphenicol) works by binding RNA, not protein. We've been treating bacterial infections by targeting RNA chemistry since the 1950s — most people just don't know that's what we're doing.

# Ribosome as Ribozyme

## Technical Core

The standard model of cellular biology: enzymes are proteins, RNA is a messenger. The ribosome is the flagship exception.

| Ribosome Component | Role | Material |
|-------------------|------|----------|
| rRNA (23S in prokaryotes; 28S in eukaryotes) | Peptidyl transferase — catalyzes peptide bond formation | **RNA** |
| Ribosomal proteins (~50+ proteins) | Structural scaffold; facilitate correct rRNA folding | Protein |
| Result | Protein synthesis | RNA does the chemistry |

## What "Ribozyme" Means Here

The 23S/28S rRNA has catalytic activity: it positions the A-site aminoacyl-tRNA and P-site peptidyl-tRNA, and directly catalyzes the nucleophilic attack forming the peptide bond. Ribosomal proteins cannot substitute.

This was confirmed by crystallography (Nobel Prize 2009: Ramakrishnan, Steitz, Yonath): the peptidyl transferase center is protein-free. All adjacent amino acids are >18 Å from the catalytic site.

## Implications

1. **RNA World support**: If RNA can both store information AND catalyze reactions, the origin of life does not require proteins to arise first. Ribosomes are a living fossil of the RNA World.
2. **Drug targets**: Antibiotics targeting ribosomes (e.g., erythromycin, chloramphenicol) bind the 23S rRNA, not ribosomal proteins — they inhibit RNA, not protein.
3. **Evolutionary conservation**: The peptidyl transferase center rRNA sequence is the most conserved sequence in all life — more conserved than any protein-coding gene.

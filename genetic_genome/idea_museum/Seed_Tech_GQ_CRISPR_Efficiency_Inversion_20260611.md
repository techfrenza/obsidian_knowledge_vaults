**Concept**: G-quadruplex structures in the target strand decrease CRISPR cleavage efficiency — the very sequences Cas9 was designed to unwind can neutralize it.

**Hook Insight**: We assume CRISPR is a programmable machine that cuts wherever you point it. But G4 structures in the target strand can hold their fold against the R-loop, partially blocking Cas9. High-stability G4s (Tm >68°C) near the cleavage site can reduce cutting efficiency by 20-50% on the G-rich strand specifically — while the complementary C-rich strand is cut normally.

**Wiki Link**: [[G-Quadruplex–CRISPR Interference]]

**Draft Hook**: Everybody says CRISPR can cut any DNA sequence. It can't. Place a sufficiently stable G-quadruplex in the target strand and Cas9 becomes a half-broken scissors — cleaving one strand but barely touching the other.

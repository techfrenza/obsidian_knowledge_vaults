# X Hook Drafts

Hook 1 (shocking reframe):
Your 46 chromosomes have 92 ends. Every single end looks like a broken DNA molecule. Your cells are constantly sensing "92 DNA breaks that need emergency repair." The only thing stopping a catastrophic repair response that fuses all your chromosomes together is a protein called TRF2. One protein. That's it.

Hook 2 (consequence):
Scientists studying chromosome instability don't need complex genetic manipulations to make chromosomes fuse. They delete one protein (TRF2) and wait a few hours. Every chromosome end gets joined to another chromosome. 92 ends → chromosome fusion catastrophe. The barrier between genomic stability and chaos is thinner than you think.

Hook 3 (evolutionary angle):
Evolution solved the "don't repair your chromosome ends" problem with a protein that sits on every chromosome end and blocks the repair machinery. But this solution creates a new problem: what if that protein is lost? Cancer cells frequently mutate or lose shelterin components — which is why chromosome rearrangements and fusions are a hallmark of cancer. The stability protein is the cancer vulnerability.

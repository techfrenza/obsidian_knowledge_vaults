# X Hook Drafts

Hook 1 (origin-of-life paradox):
To evolve accurate DNA copying, you need proteins. To make proteins, you need accurate DNA copying. Life somehow broke this circular dependency 4 billion years ago — with no enzymes, no ribosomes, nothing. The first replicating RNA molecule had to be accurate enough to exist, using only chemistry. That's the mystery at the origin of life.

Hook 2 (upper bound framing):
There's a mathematical limit on how many errors a replicating molecule can make before it ceases to exist. Exceed that limit and every copy is unrecognizable from the original — information collapse. The first life forms lived right at that edge, with no proofreading, no repair, nothing but the chemistry of the molecule itself. Modern DNA polymerases are 10,000x more accurate. Life started at the minimum viable accuracy.

Hook 3 (RNA virus connection):
HIV mutates so fast that no two virus particles in your body are genetically identical. This is intentional — high mutation rate = rapid immune escape. But HIV is already near the error threshold. Mutagenic drugs like molnupiravir push HIV just past that threshold: the virus mutates itself into extinction. Eigen's 1971 theory of error catastrophe is being used as an antiviral drug strategy.

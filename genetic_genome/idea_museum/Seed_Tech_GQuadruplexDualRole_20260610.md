# G-Quadruplex: Genome Guardian and Genome Hazard

## Technical Core

G-quadruplexes (G4s) form wherever there are runs of 3+ guanines. This is both the solution to chromosome end-protection and a source of replication and transcription stress across the genome.

## Trade-off Table

| Context | Effect | Net Impact |
|---------|--------|------------|
| Telomere 3' overhang | End-capping; blocks nucleolytic degradation and end-fusions | Beneficial |
| Gene promoters (G-rich) | Transcription regulation (can activate or block) | Context-dependent |
| Replication forks | Stalls DNA polymerase → DSBs → genome instability | Harmful |
| Mitotic chromosomes | Increases under low Mg²⁺/K⁺ → contributes to fragile sites | Harmful |

## Why This Paradox Exists

G-quadruplexes form fast (milliseconds) and are stabilized by molecular crowding and high viscosity — exactly the conditions inside a nucleus. The TTAGGG telomere repeat sequence was *selected* because it forms G4 well, not despite it forming G4.

The genome-wide cost: any G-rich sequence can spontaneously form G4, and there are an estimated 375,000 potential G4 sites in the human genome. Most must be resolved by helicases (e.g., FANCJ, RHAU/DHX36) to allow replication.

## Key Implication

Targeting G4 structures for cancer therapy (e.g., G4-stabilizing ligands to collapse cancer cell telomeres) is complicated by collateral G4 stabilization at non-telomeric sites, causing off-target genome instability.

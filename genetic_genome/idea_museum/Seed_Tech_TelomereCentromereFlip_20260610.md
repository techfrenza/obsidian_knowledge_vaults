# Telomere-Centromere Evolutionary Interchange

## Technical Core

The canonical view treats telomeres (chromosome-end caps) and centromeres (chromosome-segregation anchors) as entirely separate, independently evolved structures. The evidence says otherwise.

| Evidence | Significance |
|----------|-------------|
| *Drosophila* Y-centromere evolved from a telomere | Direct phylogenetic case of the conversion |
| CENP-A and CENP-N (centromeric proteins) are recruited to double-strand breaks | Same proteins function at both break sites and centromeres |
| Subtelomeric regions generate neocentromeres when native centromeres are deleted | Latent centromere potential in telomere-adjacent sequences |
| Proto-telomeres had dual function: end protection + chromosome segregation | The ancestral structure performed both jobs before they diverged |

## Evolutionary Model

1. Ancestral linear chromosome ends: G/C-biased retrotransposon insertions at DSB ends → proto-telomere
2. Proto-telomere: bifunctional — end-caps AND directs chromosome segregation
3. Centromere function separated to internal chromosomal regions
4. Telomere retained end-capping; centromere function relocated inward
5. Modern state: appear unrelated, but share protein machinery and evolutionary origin

## Advantages and Limitations of This Framework

**Explanatory power:**
- Explains why centromeric proteins are recruited to DSBs (ancestral telomere-associated function)
- Explains neocentromere formation potential in subtelomeric regions

**Open questions:**
- Transition mechanism from subtelomeric to internal centromere position is incompletely understood
- Budding yeast has point centromeres (sequence-defined), insects have holocentric chromosomes — the model must accommodate structural diversity

# X Hook Drafts

Hook 1 (counter-intuitive opener):
A salamander has 10 times more DNA per cell than you do. A lungfish has 40 times more. They are not 40x more complex than a human. Their cells just make smaller cuts when fixing broken DNA. Over millions of years, smaller deletions = bigger genome. Genome size is a repair fossil, not a complexity score.

Hook 2 (question):
Why does a salamander have more DNA than a human? The best current answer: because when salamander cells break their DNA and repair it, they make slightly smaller deletions. That's it. Repeated a trillion times across evolutionary time — that's the C-value paradox solved.

Hook 3 (implication):
If genome size is determined by how precisely cells repair broken DNA, then genome editing tools (CRISPR) that create breaks and allow imprecise repair are — at a tiny scale — doing what evolution did to create genome diversity. Every off-target NHEJ event is a micro-version of the process that gave salamanders 40x more DNA than us.

**Concept**: Where a gene sits in 3D space—not just its sequence—predicts how efficiently CRISPR will cut it. Crowded chromosomal neighborhoods are harder to edit.

**Hook Insight**: CRISPR prediction tools use ~425 features: nucleotide identity, melting temperature, epigenetic marks. But the single most predictive feature from a 2024 study is none of these — it is the 3D spatial density of the target site derived from Hi-C data. Sites in genomically "sparse" regions are cut more efficiently, independent of gene expression and epigenetic state.

**Wiki Link**: [[Genomic 3D Structure and CRISPR Efficiency]]

**Draft Hook**: You've been designing CRISPR guides based on sequence. But your genome is 3D — and the location of your target in 3D space predicts editing efficiency better than the nucleotides at the cut site.

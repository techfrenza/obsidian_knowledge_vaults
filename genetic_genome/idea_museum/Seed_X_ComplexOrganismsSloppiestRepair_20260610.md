# X Hook Drafts

Bacteria are better at fixing their DNA than you are.

When bacteria get a double-strand break — the most dangerous type of DNA damage — they repair it using the sister chromosome as a perfect template. Near-zero error rate.

When you get the same break, your cells mostly just glue the ends back together. No template. Lots of errors. Small insertions and deletions at every repair site.

This isn't a design flaw. It's an evolutionary consequence of having 98% noncoding DNA. When most of your genome is non-functional spacer sequence, most breaks land in territory where errors are irrelevant.

The cruel irony: this same "good enough" repair strategy is exactly why gene therapy in human cells is so hard. CRISPR can cut precisely — but then the cell's own repair machinery ignores the precision and glues things back imprecisely, 1,000 times more often than it uses the template you provided.

We're trying to do precision surgery in a body that evolved to prefer duct tape.

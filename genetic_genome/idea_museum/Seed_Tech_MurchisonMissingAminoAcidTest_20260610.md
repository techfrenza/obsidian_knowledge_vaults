# Missing Amino Acid Pattern as Universal Biogenicity Test

## Technical Core Logic

The key discriminator between ancient genuine biological material and modern contamination:
- Modern biocontaminants: contain all 20 protein amino acids (fresh biological material always does)
- Ancient fossilized biology: loses the same 12 amino acids through diagenesis, regardless of whether the source is extraterrestrial (Murchison meteorite) or terrestrial (hadrosaur fossils)

The absent amino acids in Murchison: HIS, LYS, MET, PHE, THR, TRP, TYR, ARG — the same pattern found in multiple terrestrial paleontological specimens.

## Why This Works

Different amino acids have different thermal/chemical stability:
- Unstable amino acids (aromatic side chains, sulfur-containing, positively charged) degrade preferentially over geological/cosmological timescales
- Stable amino acids (small aliphatic: GLY, ALA, VAL, etc.) persist
- This differential stability creates a predictable "amino acid decay fingerprint" that is universal across all fossilized biological material

## Trade-off vs Other Tests

| Test | Pros | Cons |
|------|------|------|
| Morphology | Visual; non-destructive | Abiotic mimics exist |
| Isotope ratios (δ¹³C, δD) | Confirms extraterrestrial origin | Requires specialized equipment |
| Enantiomeric excess | Chirality biomarker | Low ee can mislead |
| Missing amino acid pattern | Universal across taxa; detects both biogenicity AND indigenicity | Requires mass spec amino acid profiling |

## Key Implication

A sample that shows selective amino acid absence (the right 12) simultaneously proves: (1) the material was once biological, and (2) it is genuinely ancient (not a recent contaminant). This three-pronged confirmation (morphology + isotopes + missing amino acids) is currently the strongest available case for extraterrestrial life in carbonaceous meteorites.

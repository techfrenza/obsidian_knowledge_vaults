**Concept**: HMEJ (homology-mediated end joining), using just 40-48 bp of homology, achieves 20-100% knock-in rates — far exceeding the gold-standard HDR (1-5%) that requires hundreds to thousands of bp.

**Hook Insight**: The entire gene therapy field spent decades trying to optimize HDR (precise repair) because NHEJ (imprecise repair) dominates in human cells. HMEJ sidesteps this by exploiting a third pathway — single-strand annealing — that is RAD51-independent and uses almost no homology. Less homology = more efficiency.

**Wiki Link**: [[Homology-Mediated End Joining (HMEJ)]] · [[CRISPR-Cas9]]

**Draft Hook**: Gene therapy scientists spent 30 years trying to make precise DNA repair (HDR) work in human cells. A new method using barely any homology beats it by 20x — because it uses a completely different repair pathway nobody was targeting.

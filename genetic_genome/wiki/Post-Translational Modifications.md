# Post-Translational Modifications

Post-translational modifications (PTMs) are covalent chemical changes to proteins after [[Translation]]. They determine protein localization, activity, stability, and interactions.

## Major Types

| PTM | Effect |
|-----|--------|
| Phosphorylation | Activates/inactivates intracellular signaling; added by kinases, removed by phosphatases |
| Glycosylation | Affects membrane protein stability and cell-surface recognition |
| Acetylation | Modifies histone and non-histone protein transcriptional activity |
| Methylation | Regulates chromatin structure and protein–protein interactions |
| Ubiquitination | Tags proteins for proteasomal degradation (ubiquitin-proteasome system) |
| Proteolytic cleavage | Activates proenzymes (e.g., trypsinogen → trypsin); removes signal peptides |

## Regulatory Significance

PTMs expand the functional diversity of the ~20,000 human protein-coding genes far beyond their translation products. The same protein can adopt multiple functional states through combinatorial PTMs.

## PTMs and Gene Regulation Feedback

- Histone acetylation/methylation: alter chromatin accessibility, feeding back to control [[Transcription]]
- Ubiquitin-proteasome degradation: terminates [[Translation]] products to adjust protein levels

## CRISPR Connection

[[CRISPR-Cas9]]-based tools (CRISPRa, CRISPRi) use dCas9 fused to epigenetic modifiers to introduce PTM-like changes on histones, reprogramming gene expression without altering the DNA sequence.

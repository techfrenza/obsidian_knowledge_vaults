# Central Dogma

The central dogma states that genetic information flows unidirectionally from DNA → RNA → Protein. DNA sequences specify mRNA sequences through [[Transcription]], and mRNA sequences specify amino acid sequences through [[Translation]].

## Key Principle

The decoding is colinear: nucleotide order in DNA directly corresponds to nucleotide order in mRNA, which directly corresponds to amino acid order in the polypeptide. Three mRNA nucleotides (one [[Codon]]) correspond to one amino acid.

## Information Flow Steps

| Step | Process | Enzyme |
|------|---------|--------|
| DNA → RNA | [[Transcription]] | RNA Polymerase |
| RNA → Protein | [[Translation]] | [[Ribosome]] + aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases |
| pre-mRNA → mature mRNA | [[mRNA Processing]] | Spliceosome, capping enzymes |

## Key Caveat: Eukaryotes vs Prokaryotes

In [[Prokaryotic Chromosome]] cells, transcription and translation are coupled (simultaneous) because no nuclear membrane separates the two processes. In [[Eukaryotic Chromosome]] cells, transcription occurs first in the nucleus; mRNA is exported after processing, and translation occurs in the cytoplasm.

## Significance

The universality of the genetic code (same codons map to the same amino acids across virtually all organisms) is evidence that the central dogma operates identically in all life. [[CRISPR-Cas9]] exploits this dogma to edit DNA and alter downstream gene expression.

# Ghost Lineage

A ghost lineage is an extinct, unknown, or unsampled evolutionary lineage that leaves genetic traces in the genomes of extant species through [[Introgression]], but has no direct fossil or sequence record in current databases.

## Definition

An evolutionary lineage with no direct physical evidence (no fossils, no sampled genomes) but whose past existence can be inferred from:
1. **Genetic variants** in living species that don't align with any known lineage
2. **Introgression signals** that only make sense if an unsampled donor existed

## Examples

| Case | Ghost Lineage | Evidence |
|------|--------------|---------|
| Modern humans | Denisovans, Neanderthals | Ancient DNA + modern genome introgression |
| Modern dogs/wolves | Ancient wolf-like species (>2 Mya) | ASIP coat color allele phylogeny |
| Brown bears | Cave bears | Partial genomic survival in Alaskan brown bears |

## Scale of the Problem

More than 99.9% of all species that have ever lived are now extinct. Only ~1.3 million eukaryote species have been catalogued out of an estimated 8.7 million alive today; microbial diversity is many orders of magnitude larger. This means ghost lineages are not exceptions — they are the rule in any [[Phylogenetics|phylogenetic]] analysis.

## Impact on [[D-Statistic (ABBA-BABA Test)]]

Ghost lineages are the primary confound for the D-statistic (ABBA-BABA test). Introgression from a ghost "midgroup" lineage produces the same signal as introgression between sampled ingroup lineages, causing systematic misidentification of donors and recipients. With only 25% of extant species sampled, >50% of significant D-statistics may be due to ghost introgressions.

## Detection Methods

- Compare genomic variants in living relatives against expected patterns from all known lineages; unexplained variants suggest a ghost donor
- Ancient DNA sequencing (when fossils yield genetic material)
- Model-based approaches that explicitly include unsampled taxa

## Branch Length Effects

Ghost lineages also distort expected branch lengths in gene trees, potentially **reversing** published conclusions about gene flow timing. See [[Ghost Lineage Effect on Branch Lengths]] for detailed analysis.

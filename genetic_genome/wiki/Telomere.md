# Telomere

A telomere is the specialized repetitive DNA–protein complex capping each end of a linear [[Eukaryotic Chromosome]], protecting against end-degradation, end-fusion, and illegitimate recombination.

## Structure

- Sequence: tandem TTAGGG repeats (canonical; ancestral Unikont sequence)
- 3' single-strand G-rich overhang (~150–200 nt): folds into [[Noncanonical DNA Structures|G-quadruplex]] structures
- [[Shelterin Complex]] (TRF1, TRF2, RAP1, TIN2, TPP1, POT1) binds and protects ends

## Telomerase

Telomerase is a reverse transcriptase that extends the G-rich strand using its own RNA subunit as template. Without telomerase, each division shortens telomeres (the "end-replication problem"). Somatic cells lack active telomerase; cancer cells and germline cells reactivate it.

## Evolutionary Origin

Proposed mechanism: Repetitive capture of G/C strand-biased non-LTR retrotransposons at double-strand break (DSB) ends gave rise to "proto-telomeres." These initially had dual function in **end protection** (capping) and **chromosome segregation**, with centromeres evolving later from subtelomeric sequences.

## G-Quadruplex Role

The G-rich 3' overhang spontaneously folds into [[Noncanonical DNA Structures|G-quadruplex]] structures. These noncanonical structures are thought to have been selected for end-capping in early eukaryotes and remain functional in present-day telomere biology.

## Telomere-Centromere Relationship

Evidence supports evolutionary interchange between telomeres and centromeres:
- Centromeric proteins (CENP-A, CENP-N) are recruited to DSBs
- *Drosophila* Y-centromere evolved from a telomere
- Subtelomeric regions generate neocentromeres when native centromeres are deleted

## Telomere Shortening and Aging

Each somatic cell division shortens telomeres. Critically short telomeres trigger senescence or apoptosis. This ties [[Eukaryotic Chromosome]] biology to cellular aging and cancer.

## Alternative Telomere Maintenance (ALT)

In some cancers and the dipteran *Drosophila*, telomeres are maintained by homologous recombination rather than telomerase. *Drosophila* uses retrotransposon insertion (HeT-A, TART, TAHRE) instead of TTAGGG repeats.

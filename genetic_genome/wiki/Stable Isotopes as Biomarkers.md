# Stable Isotopes as Biomarkers

Stable isotopes are non-radioactive isotopes of the same element that exist in constant natural proportions but become fractionated by biological and physical processes. Their ratios serve as biomarkers for life, metabolic processes, past environments, and migration patterns.

## What They Are

- Isotopes: atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons (same proton count)
- Stable = do not decay radioactively; their ratios are preserved in samples
- Common biomarker isotopes: ¹²C/¹³C, ¹⁶O/¹⁸O, ¹⁴N/¹⁵N, ¹H/²H (deuterium)

## Isotopic Fractionation

Chemical bonds with lighter isotopes are weaker; lighter isotopes react faster. Therefore, biological and physical processes preferentially incorporate one isotope over another, creating measurable ratio shifts. Atmospheric carbon is 98.89% ¹²C and 1.11% ¹³C at equilibrium; fractionation departs from this baseline.

## Applications

| Application | Isotope System | Information |
|-------------|---------------|------------|
| Fossil/ancient biomaterial indigenicity | ¹³C/¹²C | Distinguishes extraterrestrial amino acids from terrestrial contamination |
| Palaeoceanography | ¹⁸O/¹⁶O in shells | Past ocean temperature, glacial/interglacial cycles |
| Atmospheric methane sources | ¹³C in CH₄ | Wetland vs. fossil fuel vs. wildfire origin |
| Food web tracing | ¹³C, ¹⁵N | Dietary history, migration patterns |
| Drug origin identification | Multiple | Forensic applications |
| CO₂ source attribution | ¹³C/¹²C | Fossil fuels confirmed responsible for long-term CO₂ rise |

## Measurement

Isotope Ratio Mass Spectrometry (IRMS): samples converted to CO₂ gas → ionized → accelerated through magnetic field → separated by isotope mass → collected in Faraday cups → ratio computed against a known reference standard.

## Importance to [[Chirality as Biomarker]]

Stable carbon (δ¹³C) and deuterium (δD) isotope measurements of amino acids in the Murchison meteorite proved they were extraterrestrial in origin (not contamination), validating the [[Enantiomeric Excess]] measurements as genuine biosignatures.

## Planetary Science Application: [[Lunar Volatile Depletion]]

The same isotope ratio principles apply to cosmochemistry: zinc (δ⁶⁶Zn), sulfur (δ³⁴S), chlorine (δ³⁷Cl), and potassium (δ⁴¹K) isotope fractionation in lunar mare basalts record the evaporative history of the Moon during formation. Heavy isotope enrichment in lunar samples confirms large-scale volatile loss, most likely during a lunar magma ocean phase.

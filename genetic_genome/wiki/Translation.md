# Translation

Translation is the process by which [[Ribosome|ribosomes]] decode the [[Codon|codon]] sequence of mRNA into a polypeptide chain (protein). It is the final step of [[Central Dogma|gene expression]].

## Three Phases

### Initiation
- Small ribosomal subunit (40S in eukaryotes, 30S in prokaryotes) binds the 5' cap of mRNA
- Scans mRNA for the AUG start codon (Kozak sequence in eukaryotes; Shine-Dalgarno in prokaryotes)
- Met-tRNA occupies the P site; 60S (eukaryote) or 50S (prokaryote) large subunit joins
- Initiation factors (eIFs) are released

### Elongation
- A site: incoming charged [[tRNA]] enters, anticodon pairs with mRNA codon
- P site: peptidyl-tRNA holds the growing polypeptide
- E site: deacylated tRNA exits
- Peptidyl transferase (rRNA ribozyme activity) catalyzes peptide bond formation
- GTP hydrolysis drives translocation (A→P→E shift), adding one amino acid per cycle

### Termination
- Stop codon (UAA, UAG, UGA) enters A site
- Release factors (no tRNA) mimic tRNA shape; peptidyl transferase adds water instead of amino acid
- Polypeptide released; ribosome subunits dissociate

## Ribosome Composition

| Organism | Small subunit | Large subunit | Full ribosome |
|----------|--------------|--------------|--------------|
| Prokaryote | 30S | 50S | 70S |
| Eukaryote | 40S | 60S | 80S |

## Polysome

Multiple ribosomes simultaneously translate one mRNA (5'→3') = polysome/polyribosome. Maximizes protein output per mRNA molecule.

## Post-Translation

After polypeptide release, [[Post-Translational Modifications]] (phosphorylation, glycosylation, etc.) determine final protein localization, stability, and function.

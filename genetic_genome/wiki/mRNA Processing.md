# mRNA Processing

mRNA processing (or pre-mRNA maturation) converts the primary transcript (pre-mRNA) into a mature, translatable mRNA in eukaryotic cells. Three key modifications occur before nuclear export.

## Three Core Modifications

| Modification | Mechanism | Function |
|-------------|-----------|---------|
| 5' Cap (7-methylguanosine) | Methylation of 5' end | mRNA stability; [[Translation]] initiation (cap-binding complex) |
| 3' Poly(A) Tail | Addition of ~200 adenines at polyadenylation signal | Protection from degradation; translation efficiency |
| Splicing | Spliceosome removes introns, joins exons | Generates protein-coding mRNA from pre-mRNA with noncoding introns |

## Splicing Mechanism

1. Spliceosome (snRNAs + snRNP proteins) recognizes GU (5' splice site) and AG (3' splice site) at intron boundaries.
2. Branch point adenosine attacks 5' splice site → lariat intermediate forms.
3. 3' OH of upstream exon attacks 3' splice site → intron released as lariat; exons ligated.

Alternative splicing of exons generates multiple protein isoforms from a single gene, dramatically expanding proteome diversity.

## Clinical Note

Antibodies to spliceosomal snRNA components (snRNPs) are specific markers for systemic lupus erythematosus.

## Difference from Prokaryotes

Prokaryotes (see [[Prokaryotic Chromosome]]) lack splicing. Genes are polycistronic (single mRNA encoding multiple proteins), and translation begins immediately during [[Transcription]].

## Downstream

Mature mRNA is exported from the nucleus for [[Translation]] at [[Ribosome|ribosomes]] in the cytoplasm.

# Introgression

Introgression is the stable incorporation of genetic material from one lineage into another through hybridization followed by repeated backcrossing. It is a form of horizontal gene flow across species boundaries.

## Mechanism

1. Hybridization between species (F1 hybrid offspring)
2. Repeated backcrossing with one parental species
3. Most donor genome is lost through recombination; small genomic segments from the donor persist

## Prevalence

Introgression has been documented across diverse taxa:
- Humans (from Neanderthals, Denisovans)
- Bears (polar × brown bears)
- Butterflies, fishes, plants, fungi

It appears to be a common evolutionary process in the Eukaryotic domain, and is often adaptive (e.g., Tibetan high-altitude adaptation via Denisovan EPAS1 introgression).

## Detection: [[D-Statistic (ABBA-BABA Test)]]

The D-statistic (ABBA-BABA test) is the standard method for detecting introgression. It compares frequencies of two biallelic SNP patterns (ABBA and BABA) in a 4-taxon tree. Significant D indicates gene flow.

## Ghost Introgression Problem

When [[Ghost Lineage|ghost lineages]] (extinct, unsampled species) are involved, introgression from a "midgroup" ghost produces the same D-statistic signal as ingroup introgression. Donors and recipients can be systematically misidentified. This is not a minor error: simulations show that with realistic sampling (~25% of species), >50% of significant D-statistics may be ghost-driven.

## Adaptive Value

Some introgression events confer direct fitness advantages:
- Disease resistance (cave bear DNA in brown bears)
- Pathogen immunity
- Environmental adaptation

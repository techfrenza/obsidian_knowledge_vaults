# tRNA

Transfer RNA (tRNA) is the adaptor molecule that decodes mRNA codons during [[Translation]] by bringing the correct amino acid to the ribosome. It is transcribed by RNA Pol III and requires post-transcriptional maturation.

## Structure

- Cloverleaf secondary structure (4 arms, 3 loops)
- ~73–93 nucleotides long
- 3' CCA tail: amino acid attachment site
- Anticodon loop: contains 3 nucleotides that base-pair with mRNA [[Codon]]

## Charging (Aminoacylation)

Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases (one per amino acid) catalyze attachment of the correct amino acid to tRNA via a high-energy ester bond. The energy stored drives peptide bond formation during translation elongation. Each tRNA is named for its amino acid (e.g., tRNA-Ala carries alanine).

## Wobble Base Pairing

The third base of the codon (3' position) tolerates non-Watson-Crick pairing (wobble). One tRNA can therefore recognize multiple synonymous codons for the same amino acid, enabling fewer tRNA species to decode the degenerate [[Genetic Code]].

## tRNA Maturation Steps

1. Intron removal
2. Addition of 3' CCA terminus
3. Folding into cloverleaf, stabilized by base modifications (nonconventional bases)

## Role in Ribosome

During elongation in [[Ribosome|ribosomal]] translation, charged tRNAs enter the A site, are translocated to the P site (peptide bond formation), then exit at the E site.

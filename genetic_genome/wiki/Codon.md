# Codon

A codon is a sequence of three consecutive nucleotides in mRNA that specifies either one amino acid or a start/stop signal during [[Translation]].

## Key Facts

- 64 possible codons (4³ = 4 nucleotide bases, triplet combinations)
- 61 codons encode 20 amino acids (redundancy/degeneracy of [[Genetic Code]])
- 3 stop codons: UAA, UAG, UGA
- 1 start codon: AUG (methionine)

## Reading Direction

Codons in mRNA are read 5'→3'. The ribosome (see [[Ribosome]]) translates the start codon first, then proceeds sequentially to the stop codon.

## tRNA Anticodon Pairing

Each codon is recognized by a complementary anticodon on [[tRNA]], which carries the appropriate amino acid. This codon–anticodon interaction is the physical mechanism by which the [[Genetic Code]] is decoded.

## Frameshift Significance

Inserting or deleting one or two nucleotides shifts the reading frame for all downstream codons, generating a completely different (usually nonfunctional) amino acid sequence. Three-nucleotide insertions/deletions preserve the frame (in-frame mutations).

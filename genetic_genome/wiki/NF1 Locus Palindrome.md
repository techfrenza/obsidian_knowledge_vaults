# NF1 Locus Palindrome

The NF1 intronic palindrome is a naturally-occurring ~100–200 bp near-perfect palindrome at the Neurofibromatosis 1 (NF1) gene locus on chromosome 17q11 (between exons 31 and 32). It is highly polymorphic in humans and evolutionarily conserved in primates.

## Key Facts

- Site of two documented constitutional chromosomal translocations: t(17;22) breakpoints
- Highly polymorphic: multiple alleles differing in degree of palindromy and AT-repeat length
- Two allele groups: Group A (more palindromic, unstable in *E. coli*) and Group B (less palindromic, stable)
- Group A alleles are the ones found at translocation breakpoints

## Why Standard Cloning Fails

Group A alleles (more palindromic) cannot be stably cloned in *E. coli* — sequences are deleted due to SbcCD nuclease and replication hairpin bypass (see [[DNA Palindrome]]). This means the human reference genome sequence at this locus is corrupted: the sequence deposited for the Human Genome Project matches a deletion artifact.

## Yeast-Based Solution

Cloning in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (especially *sae2* mutants) stably maintains palindromic alleles. Phi-29 rolling-circle amplification + manual Sanger sequencing (not automated) was required to read palindromic sequences.

## Evolution

Center-break palindrome modification (see [[DNA Palindrome]]) drove the divergence of human Group A vs. Group B alleles and of chimp/gorilla alleles from a common ancestral palindrome. At least 4 independent center-break events occurred since the primate radiation.

## Clinical Relevance

Palindromic variation is a risk factor for NF1 gene mutation. More palindromic alleles are enriched in chromosomal translocation. The discovery that human reference genome palindromic loci are systematically corrupted suggests unknown numbers of such loci are misrepresented in public databases.

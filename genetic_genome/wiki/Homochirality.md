# Homochirality

Homochirality is the property of a group of structurally related chiral molecules where all members share the same handedness (all L- or all D-). It is widely cited as a hallmark of life, but the claim of absolute homochirality in Earth's biosphere is overstated.

## Homochirality vs Chirality

- **Chirality**: An individual molecule's non-superimposability with its mirror image (a structural property)
- **Homochirality**: A population-level property requiring all related molecules to share one handedness label (requires an arbitrary labeling convention)

## Reality: Earth Is Not Perfectly Homochiral

Contrary to the textbook claim, Earth's biosphere uses both enantiomers:
- D-alanine: present in bacterial peptidoglycans, plants, fish, octopus, cow milk, wine
- D-serine: NMDA receptor co-agonist in mammalian brains (synthesized by serine racemase)
- L-sugars: L-arabinose is more common than D-arabinose in many plants; L-rhamnose occurs only in L-form in nature
- Many natural enzymes accept both enantiomers; >150 proteins contain both L- and D-amino acids in their backbone

## The Labeling Problem

Homochirality requires assigning "handedness" labels, which is arbitrary:
- L-cysteine under L/D convention becomes R-cysteine under CIP (R,S) rules
- Switching labeling convention can break apparent homochirality of natural amino acids
- This means **apparent homochirality depends on which labeling system is used**, not on the intrinsic chemistry

## Practical Implications for Life Detection

- High, planet-wide [[Enantiomeric Excess]] is a positive indicator
- Absence of absolute homochirality does NOT rule out life
- Mixed enantiomeric patterns may reflect: embryonic life, two competing life systems, diagenetic racemization of ancient biological material
- Detection priority: enantiomeric excess (ee) → groupable homochiral families → planet-wide distribution

## Cosmic Origin via [[Aminonitrile Precursors]]

A 2023 study proposes that L-homochirality was established not in amino acids directly but in their aminonitrile (AN) precursors during Strecker synthesis. Right-handed circularly polarized Lyman-α radiation (10.2 eV) permeating the early Milky Way preferentially forms L-ANs, which then convert to L-amino acids. This resolves the inconsistency of isovaline's inverted CD spectrum with direct UV models.

## Connection to [[Chirality as Biomarker]]

Homochirality is one component of the chirality biomarker framework, but should be evaluated alongside molecular richness, [[Enantiomeric Excess]], and structural diversity rather than as a binary criterion.

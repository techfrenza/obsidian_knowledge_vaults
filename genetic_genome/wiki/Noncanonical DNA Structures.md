# Noncanonical DNA Structures

Noncanonical DNA structures are secondary DNA conformations that deviate from the standard Watson-Crick B-form double helix. They arise from specific sequence motifs and play roles in genome stability, [[Telomere]] maintenance, and chromosomal rearrangement.

## Key Types

| Structure | Sequence Requirement | Role |
|-----------|---------------------|------|
| G-quadruplex (G4) | G-rich sequences (G≥3 runs) | Telomere end-capping; replication regulation |
| Cruciform | Palindromic/inverted repeat | DSB formation; chromosomal translocation |
| R-loop | RNA–DNA hybrid | Transcription regulation; replication stress |
| Z-DNA | Alternating purine-pyrimidine | Transcription activation |

## G-Quadruplexes

G-quadruplexes consist of stacked G-quartets: planar arrangements of 4 guanines held by Hoogsteen hydrogen bonds. They form at [[Telomere]] 3' overhangs and elsewhere in the genome.

- The ancestral telomeric repeat (TTAGGG)ₙ is especially prone to G-quadruplex formation
- G-quadruplex formation is fast (milliseconds), relevant to transcription and replication timescales
- Molecular crowding and high viscosity stabilize G-quadruplexes in cellular environments
- G4s in target strand reduce [[CRISPR-Cas9]] cleavage efficiency; see [[G-Quadruplex–CRISPR Interference]]
- G4 targeting with CRISPR-guided proteins (e.g., dCas9-nucleolin fusions) enables locus-specific G4 stabilization for therapeutic purposes

## Cruciform DNA and Palindromes (see [[DNA Palindrome]])

DNA palindromes can extrude into cruciform structures under torsional stress (negative supercoiling). Cruciforms trigger:
- Double-strand breaks (DSBs)
- Chromosomal translocations (e.g., NF1 locus palindromes at 17q11; see [[NF1 Locus Palindrome]])
- Replication errors in bacteria (palindrome instability in *E. coli*)

## Evolutionary Significance

The G/C strand bias in proto-telomeric retrotransposons suggests that G-quadruplex-forming capacity was selected for in the earliest telomere function. [[Telomere]] evolution exploited the intrinsic structural properties of G-rich sequences.

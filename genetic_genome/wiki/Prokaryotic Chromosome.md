# Prokaryotic Chromosome

A prokaryotic chromosome is a single, circular DNA molecule located in the nucleoid region of the cytoplasm — a membrane-free zone. Prokaryotes can replicate DNA during cell division, enabling faster reproduction than eukaryotes.

## Key Properties

| Feature | Prokaryote | Eukaryote (see [[Eukaryotic Chromosome]]) |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Shape | Circular | Linear |
| Number per cell | Single | Multiple |
| Location | Nucleoid (cytoplasm, no membrane) | Nucleus (membrane-bound) |
| Histones | Absent; nucleoid-associated proteins instead | Present |
| Telomeres | Absent | Present |
| Cell division dependency | DNA replication can precede division | Replication must complete before mitosis |

## Coupled Transcription-Translation

Because there is no nuclear membrane, [[Transcription]] and [[Translation]] are spatially and temporally coupled in prokaryotes. Ribosomes begin translating mRNA while it is still being transcribed. This is impossible in [[Eukaryotic Chromosome]] cells where mRNA must be exported after [[mRNA Processing]].

## Operon Organization

Prokaryotic genes are often organized into operons: clusters of functionally related genes under a single promoter producing polycistronic mRNA. Eukaryotes regulate each gene independently.

## DNA Compaction

E. coli has ~1.6 mm (4 million bp) of DNA compacted by nucleoid-associated proteins (HU, H-NS, Fis, IHF) and negative supercoiling maintained by gyrase (a topoisomerase that introduces negative supercoils). Cruciform extrusion at palindromic sequences (see [[DNA Palindrome]]) is favored by negative supercoiling in bacteria.

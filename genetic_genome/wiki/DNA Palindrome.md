# DNA Palindrome

A DNA palindrome is a sequence immediately followed by its exact inverse complement with no spacer (true palindrome), forming a hairpin or cruciform structure under torsional stress. Palindromes >200 bp cannot be cloned in *E. coli* due to instability.

## Instability Mechanisms

| Organism | Mechanism | Effect |
|----------|-----------|--------|
| *E. coli* | SbcCD nuclease cleaves hairpins; replication bypass of hairpin template | Deletion of palindromic sequence (RecA-independent) |
| *S. cerevisiae* | Low-level cruciform extrusion; SAE2 limits DSB ends | Moderate stability; tolerates palindromes > *E. coli* |
| Humans | Center-break palindrome modification via [[Nonhomologous End Joining (NHEJ)|NHEJ]] | Rapid evolution of palindromic alleles; hotspot for chromosomal translocation |

## Why Yeast Is More Tolerant

*E. coli* maintains active negative supercoiling (gyrase); once cruciform extrudes and supercoiling is relieved, gyrase re-introduces negative supercoiling, trapping the cruciform. In yeast and mammals, no gyrase equivalent exists; cruciform extrusion is reversible, limiting instability.

## Human Palindromes and Disease

Palindromes at chromosomal loci are hotspots for:
- Chromosomal translocations (e.g., t(11;22), t(17;22))
- Gene amplification in tumor cells
- Inherited chromosomal rearrangements

See [[NF1 Locus Palindrome]] for a detailed human example.

## Center-Break Modification

A mechanism of palindrome evolution where a central asymmetric deletion (non-homologous end joining) partially resolves a perfect palindrome, creating a near-palindrome. This allows palindromes to persist and diversify across primate evolution rather than being eliminated.

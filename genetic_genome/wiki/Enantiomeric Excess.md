# Enantiomeric Excess

Enantiomeric excess (ee) is a quantitative measure of the imbalance between two mirror-image enantiomers of a chiral molecule. It is the primary quantitative indicator of [[Chirality as Biomarker|chiral biomarkers]] in astrobiology.

## Definition

ee = A − B (where A and B are mole fractions of the major and minor enantiomers, respectively; A + B = 1)

Or equivalently: ee% = (A' − B')/(A' + B') × 100 (using mole quantities)

- Racemic mixture: ee = 0 (abiotic chemistry baseline)
- Pure single enantiomer: ee = 1 (theoretical maximum)

## ee Strength and Interpretation

| ee Level | Interpretation |
|---------|---------------|
| High, planet-wide | Strong positive indicator of biological mechanism |
| Moderate | Requires supporting indicators |
| Low/mixed | Does NOT rule out life; may reflect old/extinct life, embryonic life, or two competing life systems |

## Processes That Affect ee

**Abiotic (reducing ee over time):**
- Spontaneous racemization (rate depends on molecule and environment conditions)
- Circularly polarized light from cosmic events (can create small initial ee)
- Spontaneous deracemization (can amplify ee locally, but reverses randomly — should cancel planet-wide)

**Biotic:**
- Enzymatic stereospecificity maintains ee
- Spatial heterogeneity: different organisms produce different enantiomers at different locations (e.g., R-carvone in spearmint vs. S-carvone in caraway)

## Group eeg (Avnir's Proposal)

For detecting life, the group eeg averages ee across a family of structurally related molecules:
eeg = Σ(aᵢ × eeᵢ)

A high eeg for a molecular family is a positive indicator even without perfect [[Homochirality]].

## Key Meteorite Data

Murchison meteorite:
- L-alanine: >50% ee
- L-glutamic acid: >30% ee
- Stable isotope analysis (δ¹³C, δD) confirms these are indigenous (extraterrestrial), not contamination

This is consistent with a hypothesis that primordial enantiomeric preference in our solar system seeded Earth's L-amino acid predominance. The ee originated at the [[Aminonitrile Precursors]] stage under Lyman-α CPL radiation, not in the amino acids themselves. Morphological evidence from [[Murchison Meteorite Microfossils]] complements the chemical ee data.

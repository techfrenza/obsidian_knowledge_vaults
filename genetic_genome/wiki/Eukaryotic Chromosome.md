# Eukaryotic Chromosome

A eukaryotic chromosome is a linear, histone-wrapped DNA molecule housed in the membrane-bound nucleus of a eukaryotic cell. Multiple chromosomes per cell allow adaptive evolution through aneuploidy.

## Distinguishing Features

| Feature | Eukaryotic | Prokaryotic (see [[Prokaryotic Chromosome]]) |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Shape | Linear | Circular |
| Number | Multiple | Single |
| Location | Nucleus | Nucleoid (cytoplasm) |
| DNA packaging | Histones → nucleosomes → chromatin | Nucleoid-associated proteins |
| Telomeres | Yes (caps linear ends) | No |
| Replication timing | Before mitosis (S phase) | Can overlap cell division |

## Chromatin Packaging Levels

1. DNA wraps around histone octamers → **nucleosome** ("beads on a string")
2. Nucleosomes coil → **30-nm solenoid fiber**
3. Further compaction before mitosis → **250-nm chromatin fiber**

Human cells contain ~2 m (3 billion bp) of DNA per cell, all compacted to fit within the ~6-µm nucleus.

## Telomeres and Replication Limits

Each cell division shortens [[Telomere|telomeres]] (TTAGGG repeats at chromosome ends). Somatic cells have limited division capacity (Hayflick limit) due to progressive telomere shortening. [[Noncanonical DNA Structures]] (G-quadruplexes) form at telomere ends and contribute to end protection.

## Transcription-Translation Separation

Because the chromosome is in the nucleus and [[Translation]] occurs at cytoplasmic [[Ribosome|ribosomes]], [[mRNA Processing]] is required before export. This allows regulatory steps impossible in prokaryotes.

## Origin from Circular Genome

The [[Telomere|origin of linear eukaryotic chromosomes]] from an ancestral circular genome is hypothesized to have been driven by group II intron invasion during endosymbiosis, creating double-strand breaks repaired by proto-telomere formation.

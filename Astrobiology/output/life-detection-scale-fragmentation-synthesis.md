## 一致主线

Life detection in this wiki is not primarily a *finding problem* — it is a *framework-fit problem*. Every note addresses a different instrument or measurement approach, but the underlying question in all 15 cores is the same: can the detection framework be trusted to return a signal even if life is present? Assembly Theory addresses this at the molecular scale (MA > 15 = detectable complexity); Stable Isotope Analysis at the geochemical scale (fractionation persists billions of years but requires Earth-bound IRMS); Transmission Spectroscopy at the atmospheric scale (disequilibrium gases only readable in H₂-rich sub-Neptune envelopes); Perseverance at the in-situ mineralogical scale (PIXL maps redox at microscale, short of confirmation). LUCA, Photosystem II Early Evolution, and Methanogen Adaptive Evolution together establish that life builds complexity fast when conditions allow — suggesting the bottleneck is detection capacity, not life's rarity at the microbial level.

**Unified claim**: Life's detectability is bounded by the narrowness of each scale's measurement assumption, and no current mission integrates across scales.

## 涌现洞察

The 15 core notes collectively map the full lifecycle of life — from chemical precursors (LUCA, Photosystem II Early Evolution) through microbial adaptation (Methanogen Adaptive Evolution, Extremophiles) to civilizational rarity (Rare Earth Hypothesis, SETI) — and they also span every detection scale from molecular (Assembly Theory, Stable Isotope Analysis), to mineralogical (Perseverance Rover, Stable Isotope Analysis), to atmospheric (Transmission Spectroscopy, TRAPPIST-1 System). Viewing them together reveals a structural problem invisible from any single note: **the detection framework used at each scale is incompatible with the detection frameworks at all other scales**. Assembly Theory (MA > 15) would detect mirror-life molecules that Chirality as Biosignature tools would miss; Transmission Spectroscopy optimized for photosynthesis-driven atmospheric disequilibrium would miss serpentinizing chemoautotrophs on TRAPPIST-1 rocky planets; Perseverance's mineralogical PIXL approach would not apply to K2-18b. This cross-scale incompatibility is invisible from within any single note because each note discusses only one scale's framework. Only by placing all 15 in the same frame does the absence of a cross-scale integration layer become a named, structural gap.

**Why this can only be seen cross-note**: No single note says "our molecular methods, mineralogical methods, and atmospheric methods are each calibrated to different life-expressions and cannot be compared." The fragmentation is emergent from the collection.

## 知识缺口

**Unanswered question**: How should positive evidence at one detection scale update the probability of positive evidence at another? If Enceladus plume mass spectrometry returns MA > 15 molecules (Assembly Theory), does this increase the prior that TRAPPIST-1e harbors atmospheric disequilibrium? Does microbial adaptation rate from Methanogen Adaptive Evolution experiments inform the expected rate of atmospheric biosignature buildup on hycean worlds (Transmission Spectroscopy)? No note addresses cross-scale Bayesian evidence integration.

**Next exploration direction**: Create a wiki note titled `Biosignature Cross-Scale Integration` that formalizes how the CoLD Scale might be extended to handle multi-scale evidence (molecular + mineralogical + atmospheric), and whether a detection signal at one scale should trigger stepped-up search sensitivity at other scales as a coordinated strategy. This would serve as a linking hub between Assembly Theory, Transmission Spectroscopy, Stable Isotope Analysis, and the CoLD Scale, and would close the single largest architectural gap in the current wiki network.

# False Negatives in Life Detection

A false-negative result in astrobiology means failing to detect life that is actually present. This risk is systematically underrepresented in mission design compared to false-positive prevention, according to a 2026 Nature Astronomy paper (ten Kate et al., Utrecht University).

## Why False Negatives Are Underappreciated

The astrobiology community dedicates extensive effort to false-positive prevention (ruling out abiotic sources of biosignature signals), but:
- False-negative risks are not yet high on the research agenda
- Missing life could lead to premature closure of research priorities
- It could also allow policymakers to approve resource extraction on planets with undetected life — causing irreversible destruction

## Sources of False Negatives

| Source | Example |
|--------|---------|
| Preservation failure | Life existed but left no detectable traces |
| Atmospheric masking | Gas interactions cancel out biosignature signal (e.g., sink + source balance) |
| Detection method limits | Looking from above when life is under a rock |
| Instrument sensitivity | Current Mars instruments less sensitive than Earth labs |
| Confirmation bias | Only searching for life we already know; novel life forms missed |
| Pattern recognition gap | Human analysis misses non-intuitive patterns |
| Chirality mismatch | Instruments designed for L-amino acid life would miss D-amino acid ([[Mirror Life]]) organisms |
| Non-photosynthetic life | Chemoautotrophs on Europa would be missed by photosynthesis-focused search (see [[Europa Chemoautotrophy]]) |

## The Recommended Research Strategy

Ten Kate et al. recommend:
1. Combine laboratory experiments + modeling + fieldwork
2. Define specific testable hypotheses before designing instruments
3. Use AI/pattern recognition to detect anomalies beyond human intuition
4. Understand geochemical baselines before landing (know what "normal" looks like)
5. Study iron-bearing mineral oxidation anomalies on Mars (example cited: unusual oxidation patterns discovered in 2025)

## Connection to Current Mars Findings

The unusual iron-mineral oxidation found near [[Cheyava Falls and Bright Angel Formation]] is cited as an example where:
- On Earth, such oxidation patterns are only seen with life present
- On Mars, the geochemical mechanism is not yet understood
- Without further investigation, this could become a false negative

## AI as Detection Aid

Pattern recognition via AI can:
- Identify signatures humans would never recognise
- Flag anomalies for follow-up analysis
- Process far more data than manual review

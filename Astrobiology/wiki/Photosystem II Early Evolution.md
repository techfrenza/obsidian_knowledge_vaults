# Photosystem II Early Evolution

Photosystem II (PSII) is the only biological system capable of oxidizing water to molecular oxygen, the process that oxygenated Earth's atmosphere during the Great Oxidation Event (GOE, ~2.4 Ga). Molecular clock analyses of the core reaction center proteins (D1 and D2) indicate that PSII's capacity for water oxidation originated in the **early Archean** — at least 1 billion years before the most recent common ancestor of all known cyanobacteria.

## Key Proteins: D1 and D2

All oxygenic photosynthesis uses PSII, whose electron-transfer core is built from two homologous proteins, D1 and D2. Both descended from a single ancestral homodimeric precursor (D0) by gene duplication. The evolutionary chain:

1. Type I / Type II reaction center divergence (~3.5 Ga)
2. Anoxygenic (L/M) branch vs. oxygenic (D1/D2) branch divergence
3. **D0 gene duplication** → D1 and D2 (~3.2 Ga under conservative estimate)
4. Slow refinement to "standard D1" inherited by all modern cyanobacteria (~2.4 Ga)

The span between D0 (ancestral homodimer) and the emergence of standard PSII is denoted **ΔT ≈ 1.0 Ga** (range: 0.6–1.5 Ga depending on model).

## Why ΔT Must Be Large

D1 and D2 share only **29% amino acid sequence identity** — far too diverged to have evolved since the GOE if rates stayed constant. Three independent lines of evidence converge on a long ΔT:

| Evidence | Implication |
|----------|-------------|
| Slow rate of D1/D2 evolution since Proterozoic (< 1% per 100 Ma) | If constant rate, divergence predates Earth's formation — rates were faster early |
| Strong purifying selection (multi-subunit complex binding 62+ cofactors) | Cannot evolve rapidly; must have had a long slow period |
| D1/D2 divergence predates L/M divergence (anoxygenic branch evolved later) | HGT from anoxygenic to cyanobacteria hypothesis untenable |

## Structural Evidence for Pre-Heterodimerization Water Oxidation

Five structural features are shared by D1 and D2 but absent in anoxygenic L/M subunits. These are interpretable only in the context of water oxidation:

1. **Conserved N-terminus**: site for FtsH protease recognition and rapid repair (D1 half-life ≈ 30 min due to singlet oxygen damage) — repair mechanism predates D1/D2 divergence
2. **Expanded loop (helix 1-2)**: for peripheral subunit interactions needed for Mn₄CaO₅ cluster assembly
3. **Peripheral pigment pairs** (ChlZ-Car): photoprotective carotenoid-chlorophyll pairs at each side — singlet oxygen scavenging needed only with O₂ present
4. **Bicarbonate binding site** (loop helix 4-5): modulates quinone midpoint potential — function is protection during Mn₄CaO₅ photoassembly
5. **Extended C-terminus**: coordinates the Mn₄CaO₅ water-oxidizing cluster

Conclusion: water oxidation likely **predated the D1/D2 gene duplication**. The homodimeric D0 ancestor could already oxidize water, though inefficiently.

## The GOE Paradox

If PSII and oxygenic photosynthesis originate in the early Archean (~3.2 Ga), why was atmospheric oxygenation delayed until ~2.4 Ga? Possible explanations:
- Low-oxygen "whiffs" in Archean rocks consistent with localized oxygenic photosynthesis
- Oxygen consumption (sinks: reduced iron, sulfur, organic matter) exceeded production for >800 Myr
- PSII was inefficient in homodimeric form; full efficiency waited for heterodimerization

## Astrobiological Significance

- Oxygenic photosynthesis was **not inevitable** — it required ~1 Ga of molecular innovation before becoming globally significant
- The rate constraint demonstrates biological evolution has minimum timescales independent of environmental conditions
- Complex photochemical machinery (like PSII) likely has long evolutionary lead times — relevant to estimates of how quickly detectable biosignatures appear on other worlds

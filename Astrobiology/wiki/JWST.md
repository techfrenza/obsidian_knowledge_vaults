# JWST

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) is NASA's flagship infrared space observatory, launched December 2021 and operational from July 2022. It is a partnership with ESA and CSA. JWST is humanity's most powerful tool for characterizing exoplanet atmospheres.

## Key Specifications

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Primary mirror | 6.5 m diameter |
| Wavelength range | 0.6–28 μm (near-to-mid IR) |
| Location | L2 Lagrange point |
| 25% observation time | Allocated to exoplanet atmospheres |

## Instruments Relevant to Astrobiology

| Instrument | Range | Used For |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| NIRISS | Near-IR | Transmission spectroscopy |
| NIRSpec | Near-IR (1–5 μm) | Molecular detection (CH₄, CO₂) |
| MIRI LRS | Mid-IR (6–12 μm) | DMS/DMDS detection on [[K2-18b]] |

## Key Astrobiological Findings (2022–2025)

| Year | Finding |
|------|---------|
| 2022 | WASP-96 b: water, clouds, haze (first molecular profile of exoplanet) |
| 2022 | WASP-39 b: CO₂ detected for first time in exoplanet atmosphere |
| 2023 | [[K2-18b]]: CH₄ (5σ), CO₂ (3σ); hycean-consistent composition |
| 2025 | [[K2-18b]]: DMS and/or DMDS at 3σ via MIRI (first mid-IR hycean spectrum) |
| 2025 | TRAPPIST-1e: faint hints of atmosphere detected |
| 2026 | LHS 3844 b: no detectable atmosphere (bare rock) |

## Limitations

- Cannot study Earth-like planets around Sun-like stars (too dim vs. star brightness)
- Limited to M-dwarf planet atmospheres for rocky world characterization
- M-dwarf activity strips atmospheres over time → many targets may lack detectable atmosphere
- JWST is better at large, puffy atmospheres than thin Earth-like ones

## MIRI LRS Transmission Technique for K2-18b

During transit, a fraction of starlight passes through the planetary atmosphere, imprinting absorption features. MIRI captured the 6–12 μm range independently from prior NIRISS/NIRSpec observations, providing an orthogonal line of evidence for DMS/DMDS.

## Future Context

JWST will be complemented by:
- [[Extremely Large Telescope]] (ELT, 2029): direct imaging, M-dwarf planets
- Habitable Worlds Observatory (~2040s): Earth-Sun analogs

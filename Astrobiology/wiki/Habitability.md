# Habitability

Habitability describes the capacity of an environment to support life. Planetary habitability requires a precise combination of physical and chemical conditions; no single factor suffices.

## Necessary Conditions for Complex Life

| Factor | Requirement | Constraint |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| Liquid water | Must exist at surface or subsurface | Temperature + pressure window |
| Nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere | N₂ + O₂ balance needed | O₂ ≥18% for fire/technology |
| Plate tectonics | Carbon cycle regulation via CO₂ | Without it: runaway greenhouse or frozen |
| CO₂ balance | Too little → no photosynthesis; too much → runaway greenhouse | Earth's own ~200M–1B yr deadline |

## Oxygen Threshold for Technological Civilisations

At least **18% atmospheric oxygen** is required to:
- Support large complex organisms
- Enable fire (prerequisite for metallurgy and industrial development)

Below this threshold, technological civilisations cannot arise even if microbial life exists. This creates an additional filter beyond biogenesis.

## Expanded Habitability: Extreme Environments

[[Extremophiles]] demonstrate that life tolerates:
- Near-boiling temperatures (Yellowstone, ~93°C)
- Oxygen-free deep subsurface rock
- High pressure at ocean floor
- Stratospheric altitudes

These findings expand the search space to [[Europa]], [[Enceladus]], and subsurface Mars.

## Habitable Zone (HZ) Concept

- Classical HZ: distance from star where liquid water is stable on surface
- Hycean HZ: wider for [[Hycean World]] planets with H₂-rich atmospheres
- Subsurface HZ: independent of star distance (tidal heating on icy moons)

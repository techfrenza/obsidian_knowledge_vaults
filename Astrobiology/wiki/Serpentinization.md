# Serpentinization

Serpentinization is a geochemical process in which iron- and magnesium-rich minerals react with water deep underground, producing hydrogen gas (H₂) as a byproduct. It creates chemically energy-rich environments that can support microbial life in complete darkness.

## Reaction Overview

Iron-rich olivine/pyroxene minerals + H₂O → serpentine minerals + **H₂** (+ heat)

This reaction:
- Occurs in Earth's oceanic crust and continental subsurface
- Produces hydrogen without sunlight
- Creates alkaline, reduced conditions

## Astrobiology Significance

| World | Relevance |
|-------|----------|
| Earth subsurface | Model system; massive microbial biosphere lives in these fluids |
| [[Europa]] | Rocky seafloor may serpentinize under deep ocean |
| [[Enceladus]] | Seafloor-water interaction inferred from Cassini data |
| Mars subsurface | Olivine-rich crust; serpentinization may produce H₂ as energy source |

## Dual-Use Challenge: Life vs. Industry

Companies globally are racing to extract H₂ from serpentinizing rock as clean fuel (burns to water, no CO₂). However, deep microbes **also consume H₂**, creating direct competition between bioenergy extraction and the subsurface biosphere.

Understanding whether microbial H₂ consumption is a "bane or boon" for energy extraction is active research (Kashyap, CU Boulder).

## Connection to Mars

Mars has olivine-rich crust. [[Perseverance Rover]] found margin unit rocks converted to carbonates and silica — evidence of ancient water-rock alteration. Future deep drilling (>2 m) could access serpentinizing zones where microbial life might persist.

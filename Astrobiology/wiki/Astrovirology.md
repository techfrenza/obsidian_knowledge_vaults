# Astrovirology

Astrovirology is the study of viruses in the context of space exploration, life detection, and the origin of life. Viruses are the most numerically abundant biological entities on Earth, yet they are systematically excluded from most life-detection frameworks — a significant methodological gap.

## Core Argument

Viruses are obligate genetic parasites that:
- **Span all known nucleic acid types** (DNA, RNA, single-stranded, double-stranded) — no other biological entities do this
- Are present in every known environment where life occurs, including extreme analogs of other worlds
- Co-evolved with cellular life since at least [[LUCA]] (CRISPR-Cas immunity found in LUCA proteome)
- Drive horizontal gene transfer and thus accelerate evolution

Ignoring viruses in the search for life is equivalent to ignoring half of life's diversity on Earth.

## Three Knowledge Gaps (Trubl et al.)

| Gap | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| **Biogeochemical role** | Viruses kill cells, releasing nutrients; drive carbon and nutrient cycling. Effect on planetary chemistries is unmeasured |
| **Virus origins** | Whether viruses predate, coincide with, or postdate cellular life is unresolved — directly relevant to origin-of-life scenarios |
| **Life evolution and distribution** | Viruses facilitate horizontal gene transfer, enabling rapid adaptation in extreme environments |

## Relevance to Life Detection

Current [[Biosignature]] detection focuses on cellular chemistry: amino acids, lipids, proteins, DNA, metabolic gases. Viruses produce distinct molecular signatures:
- Viral capsid proteins with unique structural motifs
- High-sensitivity viral genome sequences (potentially detectable via metagenomics)
- Virus-induced changes in host cell chemistry (metabolic "redirects")

A search for life that cannot detect viral biosignatures may generate **false negatives** — a type of error discussed in [[False Negatives in Life Detection]].

## Relevance to LUCA

The Moody et al. (2024) reconstruction of LUCA's proteome found a **CRISPR-Cas immune system**, meaning:
- Viruses predate the divergence of all known life
- The earliest ecosystems included predator-prey virus–cell dynamics
- Viral evolutionary pressure was a likely driver of LUCA's genomic complexity

## Planetary Protection Implications

Life-detection missions face a dual virus concern:
1. **Contamination**: Earth viruses carried on spacecraft could be misidentified as extraterrestrial biosignatures, or could infect any life found elsewhere
2. **False negatives**: If extraterrestrial viruses exist, current instruments are not designed to detect them

## Space Environments and Human Health

- Spaceflight alters virus–host dynamics in ways Earth-based studies cannot predict
- Viruses that are latent on Earth may reactivate in microgravity or radiation environments
- Life-support systems (plants, microbiomes) may be destabilised by viral effects in space

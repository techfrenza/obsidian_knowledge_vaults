# DMS and DMDS

Dimethyl sulfide (DMS, (CH₃)₂S) and dimethyl disulfide (DMDS, (CH₃)₂S₂) are organosulfur compounds that on Earth are produced exclusively by life, primarily marine phytoplankton and bacteria. They are the leading exoplanet [[Biosignature]] gas candidates.

## Properties

| Property | DMS | DMDS |
|----------|-----|------|
| Formula | (CH₃)₂S | (CH₃)₂S₂ |
| Earth producers | Marine phytoplankton | Marine organisms |
| Earth concentration | <1 ppb | <1 ppb |
| Atmospheric lifetime (Earth) | Hours to ~1 day | Short (photochemically reactive) |
| On K2-18b (inferred) | ≳10 ppmv | ≳10 ppmv |

## Why DMS/DMDS Are Considered Robust Biosignatures

1. On Earth they are **uniquely biogenic** — no significant abiotic sources at detectable levels
2. In H₂-rich atmospheres, M-dwarf UV environment allows them to **self-shield** at high concentrations, enabling accumulation
3. Abiotic photochemical synthesis requires H₂S (precursor) + low CO₂, contradicting [[K2-18b]] observations
4. Cometary delivery cannot sustain ppm-level concentrations (molecules shock-destroyed on impact)

## Spectral Features (JWST MIRI Band)

| Wavelength | Feature |
|------------|---------|
| 6.8–8 μm | Double-peak (both DMS and DMDS overlap) |
| ~9–10 μm | Broad DMS feature |
| ~10–11 μm | Broad DMDS feature |

The spectral degeneracy between DMS and DMDS in the 6–12 μm range makes it currently impossible to distinguish them definitively. Higher signal-to-noise from additional [[JWST]] observations is needed.

## Detection on K2-18b

- 2023 (NIRISS + NIRSpec): DMS tentative hint <2σ
- 2025 (MIRI LRS): DMS and/or DMDS at 2.9–3.2σ (independent pipeline confirmation)

For [[K2-18b]], sustaining >10 ppm in steady state requires biogenic oceanic flux ≳20× Earth levels.

## Knowledge Gaps

- Absorption cross-sections for DMS/DMDS in H₂-rich atmospheres at relevant pressures are poorly constrained (currently measured only in N₂-dominated near-STP conditions)
- No laboratory experiment has yet replicated abiotic DMS production under K2-18b-like conditions at observed abundance levels
- DMDS condensation in cold stratospheres may limit detectable abundance

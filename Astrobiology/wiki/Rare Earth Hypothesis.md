# Rare Earth Hypothesis

The Rare Earth Hypothesis (or Rare Technological Civilisation model) proposes that the conditions required for intelligent technological life are so stringent and rare that such civilisations are extremely uncommon or unique in the galaxy. A 2025 study by Scherf & Lammer (Austrian Academy of Sciences) quantified key constraints.

## Core Quantitative Findings (Scherf & Lammer, 2025)

Presented at EPSC–DPS 2025 Joint Meeting, Helsinki:

| Constraint | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Nearest technological civilisation | ~33,000 light-years (far side of Milky Way) |
| Minimum civilisation lifespan (to share galaxy with 1 other) | 280,000 years |
| Minimum lifespan (for 10 coexisting civilisations) | 10,000,000 years |
| Earth's own photosynthesis deadline | 200 million – 1 billion years |

## Critical Atmospheric Thresholds

Two independent atmospheric requirements create compounding barriers:

1. **CO₂ balance**: plate tectonics must regulate carbon cycling; without it, photosynthesis fails or runaway greenhouse occurs
2. **Oxygen threshold**: ≥18% O₂ required for large organisms AND fire, enabling metallurgy and technology development

Even if microbial life is common galaxy-wide, technological life capable of fire-based civilisation is filtered by the O₂ threshold.

## Relation to Fermi Paradox

The Rare Earth model partially resolves the Fermi Paradox ("Where is everybody?"):
- If civilisations are ~33,000 light-years apart and only last ~1,000–10,000 years, the probability of simultaneity is vanishingly small
- Any existing civilisation we might contact would likely be far older and more advanced than humanity

## Unknown Factors That Could Change the Model

- Frequency of abiogenesis (life's origin)
- Frequency of life developing complexity
- Speed of intelligence arising once complexity exists

These unknowns can shift the probabilities dramatically in either direction.

## SETI Implication

A SETI null result strengthens this model. A positive result would falsify it — and would be one of the greatest scientific discoveries in history.

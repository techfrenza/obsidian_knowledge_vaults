# Jezero Crater

Jezero Crater is a 45 km impact crater on Mars near the Martian equator, landing site of the [[Perseverance Rover]]. It was once a lake fed by a river delta approximately 3–4 billion years ago, making it a high-priority site for ancient life detection.

## Geological Units

| Unit | Description | Astrobiology Relevance |
|------|-------------|----------------------|
| Crater floor | Igneous (lava flows + intrusions) | Habitability baseline |
| Delta front / top | Sedimentary; confirmed river-fed lake origin | Water-rich ancient environment |
| Margin Unit | Igneous → carbonate + silica via water-rock alteration | Water history, still poorly understood |
| Neretva Vallis | Ancient river channel cutting through margin | Access to Bright Angel Formation |
| **Bright Angel Formation** | Finest-grained mudstones; oxidized; organic-rich | Site of 2025 biosignature candidate |

## Bright Angel Formation Details

Located at the "off-ramp" from crater floor to crater rim via Neretva Vallis river channel:
- Finest-grained sediment on mission — unexpected in a river channel (possibly dam-backed deposition)
- Chemically oxidized (rusty) and leached of Mg, Ca → indicates surface weathering of source material
- Organic carbon present in mudstones but absent in conglomerates (Masonic Temple)
- Contains [[Cheyava Falls and Bright Angel Formation]] rock with biosignature minerals

## Geologic Timeline

- ~3.5–4 Gya: lake filled crater; river deposited delta
- Climate became drier and more oxidizing over time
- Current: cold, dry, irradiated surface
- Subsurface may retain ice within 1 m depth at mid-latitudes

## Why Mars Is Preferred Over Earth for Ancient Life Search

- Mars lacks plate tectonics → ancient rock record preserved (Earth constantly recycles crust)
- Jezero rocks date to 3–4 Gya, the epoch when life was emerging in the solar system
- Earth's equivalent rocks have been almost entirely destroyed

# Stable Isotope Analysis

Stable isotope analysis measures the ratios of non-radioactive isotope pairs in matter. Because living processes fractionate isotopes in characteristic ways, these ratios serve as both biosignatures and environmental proxies.

## Core Principle

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Stable isotope | Non-radioactive; does not decay over time |
| Isotopic fractionation | Change in isotope ratio during chemical/physical processes |
| Lighter bonds weaker | Bonds with lighter isotopes break more easily → lighter isotope preferentially incorporated |
| Isotope ratio mass spectrometry (IRMS) | Instrument measuring ratios (e.g., ¹³C/¹²C, ¹⁸O/¹⁶O) |

**Key isotope pairs used in astrobiology**: H/D, ¹²C/¹³C, ¹⁴N/¹⁵N, ¹⁶O/¹⁸O, ³²S/³⁴S

## Processes That Leave Isotopic Signatures

| Process | Isotope Used | Signature |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| Photosynthesis | C (¹²C/¹³C) | Preferential uptake of ¹²CO₂; biogenic carbon is ¹³C-depleted |
| Sulfate reduction (microbial) | S (³²S/³⁴S) | Organisms preferentially reduce ³²SO₄; sulfides are ³²S-enriched |
| Iron reduction (microbial) | Fe (⁵⁴Fe/⁵⁶Fe) | Fractionation during Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺ reduction by microbes |
| Temperature change | O (¹⁶O/¹⁸O) | Cold water incorporates more ¹⁸O; proxy for paleoclimate |
| Decomposition | C, N | Isotopic signatures passed through food webs |

## Relevance to Mars Biosignature Assessment

For the [[Cheyava Falls and Bright Angel Formation]] potential biosignature (2025):
- **Fe isotopes**: paired analysis of Fe in mudstone vs. vivianite vs. greigite would test whether microbial Fe-reduction created the redox gradient
- **S isotopes**: ³²S/³⁴S ratio in greigite vs. surrounding sulfate would distinguish microbial sulfate reduction (strong fractionation) from abiotic high-temperature reduction (different signature)
- **C isotopes**: organic carbon δ¹³C vs. carbonate δ¹³C would reveal whether carbon was metabolically processed
- These analyses **require Earth laboratory instruments** — beyond current rover payload capability

## Evidence Discrimination Power

Stable isotopes are particularly powerful for discriminating biological from abiotic origins because:
1. Biology's fractionation magnitude is **predictable** from known metabolic pathways
2. Multiple isotope systems in the same rock provide **internally consistent** tests
3. The isotopic signature survives preservation for **billions of years** (used on Earth's oldest Archean rocks)

## Detection Method

The Isotope Ratio Mass Spectrometer (IRMS) process:
1. Sample converted to CO₂ gas
2. Ionised by filament in high-vacuum chamber
3. Accelerated by 10,000V through focusing plates
4. Magnetic field separates ions by mass (lighter ions bend more)
5. Collector cups count ions by mass; software computes ratio vs. reference standard

## Applications Beyond Mars

- **Meteorites**: stable isotopes prove indigenous vs. terrestrial contamination (Murchison L-amino acid excess verified by isotopes — see [[Chirality as Biosignature]])
- **Paleoceanography**: ¹⁸O/¹⁶O in shells reconstructs ocean temperature history across glacial cycles
- **Atmospheric methane**: ¹³C/¹²C separates biogenic vs. thermogenic vs. fossil-fuel methane sources
- **Food web tracing**: isotopic signatures pass through trophic levels

# Mars Exploration History

Mars has been the primary planetary target for life detection since the 19th century. The search has progressed through five distinct phases, each expanding detection capability.

## Phase 1: Telescopic Speculation (19th century)

- Herschel: polar caps vary seasonally → atmosphere + water
- Schiaparelli + Lowell: "canals" (optical illusion; brain connects dots)
- Result: popular imagination of Martian civilisation; scientific basis: none

## Phase 2: Early Flybys — Mars is Dead (1960s)

- Mariner 4, 6, 7 flybys: imaged only the heavily-cratered terrain
- Conclusion: Mars is a dead, Moon-like world
- Error: flybys missed the geologically active regions

## Phase 3: Global Mapping + Water Evidence (1971–2000)

- Mariner 9 (1971): first full global map; revealed Valles Marineris, ancient river channels
- Viking landers (1976): three life-detection experiments; two negative; one (labeled release) debated but likely due to perchlorates
- Mars Global Surveyor, Pathfinder: confirmed ancient water flows
- ALH84001 meteorite announcement (1996): fossilised bacteria claims → subsequently disputed

## Phase 4: Follow the Water (2004–2020)

- MER Spirit + Opportunity: found sulfate minerals confirming ancient liquid water
- Curiosity (2012–present): confirmed habitable ancient lake in Gale Crater; detected organics; 2026 finding of 21 diverse organic molecules — see [[Curiosity Rover Organic Molecules]]
- InSight (2018–2022): first seismic record of Mars; detected magnitude-5 "marsquake"; ended December 2022
- Radar: liquid water signatures beneath southern polar ice cap (hotly debated)

## Phase 5: Seek the Signs of Life (2021–present)

- [[Perseverance Rover]] (2021–): first rock-core sampling mission
- [[Jezero Crater]]: confirmed river delta and ancient lake
- [[Cheyava Falls and Bright Angel Formation]]: potential biosignature (2025)
- [[Mars Sample Return]]: planned to bring rocks to Earth for definitive analysis

## Deepest Drilling Milestone

As of 2025, deepest Mars drilling: **7 cm** (2.8 inches). Life-detection requires ≥2 m to get below surface radiation sterilization zone. InSight's HP3 probe failed to reach target depth of 5 m due to lack of soil friction.

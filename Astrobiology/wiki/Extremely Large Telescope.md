# Extremely Large Telescope

The Extremely Large Telescope (ELT) is ESO's ground-based optical/infrared telescope under construction at Cerro Armazones, Chile, due online in 2029. With a 39-meter primary mirror, it is the largest optical telescope ever built and will be capable of directly imaging exoplanet atmospheres.

## Key Specifications

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Primary mirror | 39.2 m |
| Type | Ground-based optical/infrared |
| Location | Cerro Armazones, Chile (~3,046 m altitude) |
| Expected completion | 2029 |
| Adaptive optics | Deformable mirrors counteract atmospheric turbulence |

## Comparison to JWST

| Capability | JWST | ELT |
|-----------|------|-----|
| Observation method | Transmission spectroscopy (transit) | Direct imaging |
| Target planet host | M dwarfs | M dwarfs (near-term); Sun-like stars (future) |
| Atmosphere access | Large puffy atmospheres best | Thin Earth-like atmospheres accessible |
| Limitation | Cannot separate planet from bright Sun-like stars | Ground-based atmospheric interference |

## Why ELT Matters for Life Detection

JWST has found that most M-dwarf rocky planets may lack detectable atmospheres (stripped by stellar activity). ELT's direct imaging approach:
- Can detect **thinner atmospheres** that JWST misses
- Builds up signal over months–years without needing transit alignment
- May be the **only avenue for exoplanet life detection** for the 2030s decade

## Giant Magellan Telescope (GMT)

Companion project: GMT at 24.5 m, due before 2030. Also uses deformable mirrors for adaptive optics. Smaller than ELT but complementary southern-hemisphere coverage.

## Habitable Worlds Observatory (HWO)

NASA's planned flagship space telescope for the 2040s, specifically designed to:
- Study Earth-sized planets around Sun-like stars (JWST cannot)
- Survey dozens of true "Earth twin" candidates
- Detect atmospheric biosignatures in disequilibrium chemistry

HWO will confirm or refute whether life is common at Earth-Sun-analog planets — the closest match to our own origin.

## Future Complement: Square Kilometre Array

SKA (due 2028): thousands of radio antennas across two continents. Will dramatically expand [[SETI]] radio search space.

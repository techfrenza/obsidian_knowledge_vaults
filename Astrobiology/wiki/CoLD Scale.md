# CoLD Scale

The Confidence of Life Detection (CoLD) scale is a 7-benchmark framework developed to assess the evidentiary weight of potential biosignature detections. It provides a standardized progression from initial signal to confident attribution to life.

## The 7 Benchmarks

| Step | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| 1 | Interesting potentially biological signal detected |
| 2 | Contamination ruled out as source of signal |
| 3 | Signal originates from a plausibly habitable environment |
| 4 | All known non-biological processes excluded as source |
| 5 | Independent detection with different method/instrument |
| 6 | Signal confirmed as definitively biological |
| 7 | Highest confidence — life is the only explanation |

## Current Cases

| Case | CoLD Step | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| [[Cheyava Falls and Bright Angel Formation]] | ~Step 3 | Contamination ruled out (Step 2); habitable environment established (Step 3); non-biological exclusion incomplete (Step 4) |
| [[K2-18b]] DMS/DMDS | Step 1–2 | Signal at 3σ; independent instrument confirmation; abiotic exclusion incomplete |
| Mars ALH84001 meteorite | ~Step 1 | Features debated; community leans non-biological |

## Why the Scale Matters

The scale clarifies that:
- "Potential biosignature" ≠ "evidence of life"
- A 5σ statistical result does not constitute life discovery without contextual exclusion of abiotic sources
- Discovery is a **process**, not a single moment

For [[K2-18b]], even a 5σ detection would still require ruling out unknown geological or photochemical processes on an alien world.

## Standards of Evidence Companion

The CoLD scale is used alongside the **Standards of Evidence** framework (Meadows et al.) for:
- Assessing whether data actually addresses "Are we alone?"
- Quantifying confidence placement in signals

# SETI

The Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence (SETI) is the systematic scientific search for technosignatures — observable evidence of intelligent extraterrestrial civilisations. It differs from mainstream astrobiology in targeting complex, technology-capable life rather than microbial life.

## Methods

| Method | Target | Status |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Radio signal search | Directed beacons or accidental leakage | Active (Breakthrough Listen) |
| Optical SETI | Laser pulses or signals | Active |
| Technosignature search | Atmospheric industrial markers | Emerging |
| Megastructure search | Dyson spheres etc. | Early exploration |

## Current Status

- Within ~100 light-years: no powerful directed beacons detected
- **Breakthrough Listen** (backed by $100M initiative): scanning further, looking for leakage signals
- **Square Kilometre Array (SKA)**: due online 2028; will dramatically expand radio search sensitivity

## SETI vs. UFO Policy Confusion

A Trump administration order (2026) to release government UFO files generated public confusion conflating alien visitation claims with scientific SETI. SETI Institute CEO Bill Diamond noted:
- Statistical probability of life elsewhere is extremely high
- No credible scientific evidence of alien visitation to Earth exists
- Political rhetoric does not advance the scientific search

## Civilisation Rarity and SETI Implications

Per the [[Rare Earth Hypothesis]] analysis (Scherf & Lammer, 2025):
- Nearest technological civilisation may be ~33,000 light-years away
- For SETI to detect anything, that civilisation must last ≥280,000 years
- A null SETI result strengthens the rare-civilisation model
- A positive result would be the most significant scientific discovery in human history

## Technical Core Logic

Serpentinization produces H₂ by reacting iron-rich minerals with water deep underground. Microbes in Earth's subsurface have evolved to consume this H₂ as their primary energy source — a metabolic strategy that likely exists on Europa and Enceladus too.

Energy companies are now targeting the same serpentinizing rock formations for "geological hydrogen" extraction (burns to water, zero CO₂). The astrobiology research that mapped these environments also quantified the microbial H₂ consumption rates — revealing direct competition.

## Trade-off Table

| Stakeholder | Interest | Outcome |
|-------------|---------|---------|
| Energy industry | Extract H₂ for clean fuel | Deplete subsurface microbial energy source |
| Astrobiologists | Study analog for Europa/Enceladus life | Industrial extraction destroys the model system |
| Microbes (Earth/ocean worlds) | Consume H₂ for metabolism | Outcompeted or eliminated by extraction |

## Depth Insight

The field studying whether Mars or Enceladus could harbor life in serpentinizing rocks is the same field now being asked whether extracting H₂ from Earth's serpentinizing rocks would kill the organisms living there. Astrobiology's most tractable alien-life analog is also its most commercially contested.

## Technical Core Logic

Isotope Ratio Mass Spectrometry (IRMS) measures ¹³C/¹²C, ³²S/³⁴S, and ⁵⁴Fe/⁵⁶Fe ratios to a precision that discriminates biological from abiotic fractionation with high confidence. Biology's fractionation magnitude is predictable from metabolic pathways; multiple isotope systems in the same rock provide internally consistent tests. The signature survives for billions of years.

For the [[Cheyava Falls and Bright Angel Formation]] biosignature candidate (CoLD Step 3 as of 2025):
- Fe isotopes would test whether microbial Fe-reduction created the vivianite/greigite redox gradient
- S isotopes would distinguish microbial sulfate reduction from abiotic high-temperature reduction
- C isotopes would reveal whether organic carbon was metabolically processed

**None of these analyses are possible with current rover hardware.** They require [[Mars Sample Return]].

## Trade-off Structure

| Approach | Capability | Limitation |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| PIXL + SHERLOC on Perseverance | Elemental mapping + Raman spectroscopy | Probabilistic; cannot rule out abiotic mimics |
| IRMS on Earth | Near-definitive isotopic discrimination | Physically impossible on Mars, requires sample return |
| Waiting for sample return | Full analytical power | 10+ year timeline; politically vulnerable |

The epistemological gap: rover science can accumulate consistent evidence at CoLD Steps 2–4, but cannot cross the step that would move global scientific consensus from "candidate" to "confirmed."

Concept: We are spending billions on missions that may be systematically designed to miss life — and this is a known, quantified risk that the field is not yet addressing.

Hook Insight: The 2026 Nature Astronomy paper by ten Kate et al. formally establishes that false negatives — missing life that's there — are under-researched compared to false positives. Current missions have no formal false-negative risk framework. Worse, missing life could lead policymakers to approve asteroid/planetary mining that irreversibly destroys an undetected biosphere. The entire astrobiology field has optimized for "don't claim life incorrectly" and nearly ignored "don't miss life."

Draft Hook: The astrobiology field has spent decades building defenses against crying "we found life" too soon. It almost forgot to ask: what if we're looking in the wrong place entirely?

## Technical Core

Carbon-silicon symmetry is superficially compelling: both are tetravalent, both form chains. But the Si–O bond (452 kJ/mol) is far stronger than Si–Si (222 kJ/mol), so in any aqueous environment silicon oxidizes immediately to SiO₂ — an insoluble ceramic, not a soluble metabolite. Carbon's "waste product" (CO₂) is a gas that diffuses away; silicon's waste product is a rock. Life cannot exhale rocks.

The counter-intuitive implication: searching for silicon-based life on ocean worlds is the wrong question. The right question is whether *alternative solvents* (sulfuric acid on Venus, liquid methane on Titan) could stabilize a wider range of organosilicon compounds.

## Trade-off

| Carbon | Silicon |
|--------|---------|
| Forms 10 million+ organic compounds in water | Forms SiO₂ ceramic mesh in water |
| CO₂ is gaseous — easily metabolized and excreted | SiO₂ is solid — metabolically inert |
| Rare-ish (~0.02% of Earth's crust) | Second most abundant element in crust (~28%) |
| Produces biodiversity | Produces sand |

The element that writes the universe's chemistry textbooks cannot write the chemistry of life — in the solvent that covers 71% of Earth's surface.

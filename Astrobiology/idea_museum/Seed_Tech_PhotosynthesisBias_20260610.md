## Technical Core Logic

The standard atmospheric biosignature ladder (O₂, O₃ as primary targets; DMS, N₂O as secondary) assumes photosynthesis or photosynthesis-derived oxygen chemistry. This was rationally derived from Earth's surface biosphere — where photosynthesis dominates by biomass and atmospheric impact.

But [[Europa Chemoautotrophy]] and deep-sea vent biospheres demonstrate that entire ecosystems — complex, diverse, multi-trophic — can operate on:
- Serpentinization H₂ (hydrogenotrophy)
- Volcanic sulfate (sulfate reduction)
- Radiolysis oxidants (iron/methanotrophy)

None of these produce atmospheric O₂ or O₃ at detectable levels. A planet entirely powered by chemosynthesis could have:
- A reducing atmosphere with no O₂ signal
- CH₄ and H₂S as dominant atmospheric gases (currently treated as abiotic or volcanic)
- No detectable DMS (which requires marine phytoplankton-like organisms)

## Trade-off Table

| Search Strategy | Targets Found | Targets Missed |
|----------------|--------------|---------------|
| O₂/O₃ biosignature search | Photosynthetic biospheres | Chemoautotrophic biospheres |
| DMS/DMDS search (JWST) | Marine biota on hycean worlds | Deep subsurface metabolisms |
| Current combined pipeline | Surface/atmospheric life | Any life below a radiation-opaque ice shell |

The irony: the [[Extremophiles]] research that expanded the definition of life on Earth has not yet been fully incorporated into the list of biosignatures we look for on other worlds.

## Technical Core

Ammonia-water mixtures remain liquid down to −100°C, giving icy moon interiors a mechanism to sustain oceans that pure water ice would not. But NH₃ diffuses passively through cell membranes, raising intracellular pH and disrupting the proton motive force that powers ATP synthesis. The critical divide:

- Below ~0.02 M: bacterial survival possible
- Above ~0.5 M: lethal to all known life
- Enceladus: 0.0181 M (within survival range — just)
- Titan (15% NH₃ model): 0.96–9.6 M (likely lethal)

Enceladus sits at the knife-edge of its own habitability. Titan probably sits on the wrong side of the blade.

## Trade-off

| Role | Concentration | Effect |
|------|--------------|--------|
| Antifreeze | Any | Liquid ocean maintained |
| Nitrogen source (prebiotic) | Any | Amino acid/nucleotide precursor |
| Cell membrane disruptor | >0.02 M (sensitive species) | Proton motive force destroyed |
| Lethal poison | >0.5 M | No known organism survives |

The same measurement — ammonia concentration — is the primary variable for assessing whether an icy moon ocean can sustain life *and* whether it destroys life. Cassini gave us one data point for Enceladus; Dragonfly may give us one for Titan by 2034.

## Hook Drafts

Saturn's moon Enceladus is doing something extraordinary: it is actively ejecting its subsurface ocean into space through geysers at its south pole. That ocean contains water, organics, silica, salts, and phosphorus — every ingredient checked on the life list. A spacecraft could fly through the plume and collect samples without landing, without drilling, without a sample return mission.

We have already done this. Cassini flew through the plumes. It just wasn't designed to detect life directly.

The astrobiology community has known about this for nearly 20 years. The priority mission to go back, land-free, sample-ready — has never been funded.

Europa gets the missions. Enceladus has the free samples.

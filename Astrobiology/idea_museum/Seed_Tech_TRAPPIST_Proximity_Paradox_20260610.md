## Technical Core Logic

M-dwarf habitable zones require close orbital distances (short period, strong transit signal). This produces the detection advantage that makes TRAPPIST-1 the premier JWST target. But:

- Close orbits expose planets to intense stellar flares and UV
- M-dwarfs are magnetically active and produce coronal mass ejections
- JWST data on TRAPPIST-1b: likely atmosphere-stripped
- TRAPPIST-1e (faint hints, Sept 2025): still uncertain

The same proximity that produces a strong, frequent transit signal for JWST also drives the atmospheric stripping that may have rendered these planets barren.

## Trade-off Table

| Property | Detection Value | Habitability Impact |
|----------|----------------|-------------------|
| Close orbit | Frequent transits, strong signal | Stellar radiation strips atmosphere |
| M-dwarf host | Cool star → habitable zone is close | High flare activity, UV bombardment |
| Short year (~10 days) | Many transits per year | Tidal locking possible; climate extremes |

## Structural Implication

This is not just a practical challenge — it is a selection bias problem. Our observational pipeline preferentially detects planets in conditions hostile to habitability. The most detectable planets are not a random sample; they are filtered toward high-radiation, close-orbit environments. Life-bearing planets around Sun-like stars (wider habitable zones, lower radiation) are currently near-undetectable.

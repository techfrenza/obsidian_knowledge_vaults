## Hooks Draft

Earth-like planets (rocky, nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere, ~1 Earth radius) produce spectroscopic signals so small they are essentially undetectable with any telescope planned for this decade. Their thin atmospheres barely imprint on starlight. Finding O₂ on a true Earth twin requires a mission that doesn't exist yet.

Hycean worlds (sub-Neptune, H₂-rich, global ocean) are different: the H₂ atmosphere has lower molecular weight, giving it a larger scale height. The bigger planet produces a larger transit depth. Both effects amplify the transmission signal by 5–10x. DMS at >10 ppm on K2-18b would be undetectable on a true Earth analogue with JWST.

The irony: the search strategy that felt most natural — find a planet like Earth — is the strategy least likely to find anything.

Hook for X: We spent 20 years searching for Earth-sized planets because they're "most like us." Then we pointed JWST at a 2.6× Earth-radius ocean world covered in hydrogen — and detected a possible biosignature in its first observation. The least Earth-like planets are the easiest to read.
